A foreign distributor sells better when it receives material written for its market: in its language, with examples that speak to its customers, tied to its country's tenders and education guidelines. Preparing tailored product sheets for every distributor by hand, though, does not scale: with many foreign distributors and a wide catalogue, the work never ends.

In the case behind this article, one person did that work with ChatGPT, one product and one distributor at a time. It worked, but it was not repeatable, and nobody could tell where any given claim came from.

**In brief:**
- A foreign distributor sells better with material written for its market, but preparing it by hand for each one stops being sustainable as distributors and catalogue grow.
- Sheets can be generated from item master data and the ERP, adapting language, examples and selling points to each distributor, without inventing technical data.
- AI agents can monitor customers' websites, tenders and guidelines, and flag the opportunity with the right item and a link to the source.
- In public supply contracts, EU rules apply in 2026-2027 above €140,000 for central government authorities and above €216,000 for other contracting authorities, according to the European Commission.
- Making manual ChatGPT work repeatable pays off when there are many markets; with a few distributors, ChatGPT is enough.

## Why one sheet for everyone sells little

A standard product sheet is written for nobody in particular. Every distributor receives the same one, and each has to translate it, adapt it and connect it on its own to what matters in its market:

- **the language**, and the way schools describe what they buy;
- **the education guidelines** of its country;
- **the tenders** schools use to buy;
- **its own customers**, each with their own needs.

If the distributor has no time to do it, the product sinks to the bottom of its catalogue.

## What we designed

For a manufacturer of educational products that sells to schools through foreign distributors, we designed a system in two parts.

**Tailored sheets.** The system starts from item master data and the ERP, where product data already lives, and generates one sheet per distributor: in its language, with examples and selling points suited to its customers.

**Agents watching the sources.** AI agents keep an eye on public sources: customers' websites, tenders, guidelines. When they find an opportunity, such as a supply tender, they flag it to sales together with the relevant catalogue item.

In both cases sources stay traceable: you know which master data field a feature comes from and which page an opportunity comes from.

## Public tenders: where to look

Public schools buy under public procurement rules, and thresholds decide which rules. According to the [European Commission](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/public-procurement/legal-rules-and-implementation/thresholds_en), EU rules apply to contracts above certain values, which for 2026-2027 are:

- **€140,000** for supplies bought by central government authorities;
- **€216,000** for supplies bought by other contracting authorities, which the directives call "sub-central".

Above the thresholds, tenders are found on [TED](https://ted.europa.eu/), the EU tenders portal that serves as the Supplement to the Official Journal of the EU. Below them, national rules apply, country by country. An agent that monitors tenders has to watch both: it is the same work we describe in [an AI agent for the tender office](/en/blog/ai-agent-tender-bid-office/), seen from the side of a company that sells through distributors.

## Where AI helps, and where it does not

AI helps in two places: writing and adapting text, and reading messy public sources. In both, the risk is the same: a plausible claim that is wrong.

That is why the two have to be kept apart. Technical data comes only from the master data, never from the model; AI writes the text around it. And every flagged opportunity carries a link to its source, so sales can check it before acting.

If the master data is incomplete, the system cannot invent what is missing: the base has to be fixed first. We cover this in [getting your company data ready for AI agents](/en/blog/data-readiness-ai-agents/).

## When you don't need it: sometimes ChatGPT is enough

Straight talk, before you spend anything:

- **Few distributors and a small catalogue**: ChatGPT and a good shared sheet template cost less than a system.
- **Distributors don't use the material they receive**: the problem is not the sheet, it is the commercial relationship.
- **The master data is unreliable**: the sheets would repeat the errors, in several languages.

If you have many distributors, a wide catalogue and someone spending their days copying and pasting between the ERP and ChatGPT, the numbers add up quickly.
