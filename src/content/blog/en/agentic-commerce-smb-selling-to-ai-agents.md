For years your buyer has been a person: opens Google, clicks, reads the product page, adds to cart. The actor is changing. Increasingly, the one searching, comparing and shortlisting is an **AI agent** acting for your customer.

The operational question isn't philosophical. It's: when your customer's agent reads your catalog, does it understand what you sell, at what price, and whether it's available? Or are you handing it data a machine can't read?

**Key takeaways:**
- Agentic commerce is the transactional layer of AI search: not just being cited, but being **read and chosen** by an agent that buys or recommends buying for the customer.
- The useful work for an SMB in 2026 isn't building a selling agent, it's making product feeds, prices and availability **machine-readable and reliable**.
- It's distinct from GEO (being cited for leads): here what counts is structured data, prices consistent across systems, and clear return terms.
- The concrete first step is an audit of product data and site architecture, from **€2,000**, without rebuilding the e-commerce from scratch.
- If your product data is dirty or prices are misaligned, agentic commerce should **wait**: clean the data first, then sell to agents.

## What agentic commerce is

Agentic commerce is a purchase mediated by an AI agent: software that, on behalf of a person, searches for products, compares options, evaluates price and availability, and in some cases completes the transaction. Your counterpart is no longer just the user browsing your site, but also the agent that must be able to read and trust your data to include you in the choice.

Bluntly: the product page written to persuade a human is no longer enough. You need a version of the data a machine can read without ambiguity.

## Why it matters for your SMB now (and why not)

Yes, if you have a B2C or B2B online catalog. Shopping assistants and AI engines already recommend products; fully autonomous checkout is early, but the selection phase is already real. If the agent can't read you, it won't consider you.

No, or not yet, if you sell only via tenders, direct relationships, or with no public catalog. There the urgency is low and there are better priorities.

The honest point: the work agentic commerce requires, clean structured data, consistent prices, reliable availability, pays off **regardless**, even if agents vanished tomorrow. It's not a bet on the future, it's data hygiene with an upside.

## Agentic commerce isn't the GEO you already do

This boundary matters, otherwise you pay twice for the same thing.

**GEO** (or AI search optimization) helps you get cited by ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews for content and leads. It's work on findability and authority. We cover it in the [AI Search Optimization](/en/ai-search-optimization) service.

**Agentic commerce** is the layer below, the transactional one:

- structured product data (machine-readable attributes, categories, variants)
- machine-readable prices consistent across e-commerce, ERP and feeds
- real-time availability, not "ask the supplier"
- explicit return, shipping and warranty terms

An agent cites whoever it finds. But it recommends buying, or buys, from whoever it can **read and verify** without risk. Two different gates.

## The 4 blocks to fix

### 1. Machine-readable product feed

Consistent attributes, standard categories, clear variants, descriptions that answer the buyer's real questions. If your feed lives only inside a graphic template, an agent struggles to extract it.

### 2. Prices consistent across systems

The price on the site, in the ERP (TeamSystem, Zucchetti, Odoo) and in external feeds must match. An agent finding three different prices drops you for risk. It's a sync problem, not a marketing one.

### 3. Reliable availability

"In stock" must genuinely mean in stock. An agent recommending an out-of-stock product burns the customer's trust and your reputation with the agent itself.

### 4. Transparent terms

Returns, shipping, warranty, timelines. The agent also assesses risk for its user. Explicit, machine-readable terms get you shortlisted; hidden terms cut you out.

## The role of agentic customer support

When the purchase is agent-mediated, after-sales changes. Questions about order status, returns and replacements increasingly come from an automated channel that wants structured answers. A [customer support](/en/customer-support) layer that exposes order status and policies readably reduces friction and human escalations. Same principle as the feeds: clear data, less friction.
