Every year a founder stops me on the same sentence: *"the project makes sense, but there's no budget this year"*.

In 2026 that sentence has a concrete way out, at least for part of the project. Italy's MIMIT **Cloud and Cybersecurity** voucher covers **50% of eligible spend as a non-repayable grant**, up to a €20,000 contribution. And the cloud and security part of an AI agent project can fall inside it, if you buy it from suppliers on the Ministry's list.

Careful: it's not a magic shortcut. It's a tool with precise rules. Here's the honest read.

## What is the MIMIT Cloud and Cybersecurity Voucher 2026?

The Cloud and Cybersecurity Voucher 2026 is an incentive from MIMIT (Italy's Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy), run with Invitalia, for SMEs and self-employed professionals across Italy with a connection of at least 30 Mbps download. It covers **50% of eligible spend as a non-repayable grant**, up to a €20,000 contribution, for cloud and cybersecurity products and services on the Ministry's list, bought from registered suppliers. The spending plan must be at least €4,000. The budget is €150 million, of which about €71 million is reserved for Abruzzo, Basilicata, Calabria, Campania, Molise, Apulia, Sardinia and Sicily. Applications can be filled in from 20 October 2026 and submitted from 10 November 2026 to 20 January 2027, reviewed in chronological order until the funds run out. The rules are on [Invitalia's page](https://www.invitalia.it/incentivi-e-strumenti/voucher-cloud-e-cybersecurity), the [MIMIT page](https://www.mimit.gov.it/it/incentivi/sostegno-alla-domanda-di-servizi-di-cloud-computing-e-cyber-security) and in the [directorial decree of 4 August 2026](https://www.mimit.gov.it/it/normativa/decreti-direttoriali/decreto-direttoriale-4-agosto-2026-voucher-cloud-cybersecurity-termini-e-modalita-di-presentazione-delle-domande-di-agevolazione), all in Italian.

**Key takeaways:**
- The Cloud and Cybersecurity voucher covers **50% as a grant**, up to a **€20,000** contribution, on cloud and security products and services, for SMEs and self-employed professionals.
- It does not fund "AI agents" as a standalone category: it only pays for what is on the **MIMIT list**, bought from registered suppliers. Custom development isn't there; the infrastructure the agent runs on can be.
- Applications are submitted from **10 November 2026** to 20 January 2027 and reviewed in order of arrival: whoever arrives with offers and documents ready wins.
- Software development and training stay out: cover them with other incentives or your own budget.
- An incentive doesn't justify a project without ROI: baseline first, then the application.

## What the voucher actually covers

The MIMIT measure exists to push SMEs and professionals toward the cloud and toward a better security posture. The decree lists five spending families, all to be picked from the list of qualified suppliers:

- **Security hardware**: firewalls, routers and switches, intrusion detection and prevention systems.
- **Security software**: antivirus and antimalware, network monitoring, data encryption, SIEM, vulnerability management.
- **IaaS and PaaS cloud**: virtual machines, storage and backup, VPN and DDoS protection, databases.
- **SaaS cloud**: accounting, HR management, ERP and business productivity, including with artificial intelligence features, CMS and e-commerce, CRM and virtual switchboards.
- **Configuration, monitoring and support** of the services bought, up to 30% of the spending plan.

You buy outright or through a subscription of at least 24 months. Excluded: staff training, products that replicate ones you already use, extra licences or seats on software you already have, and version upgrades without a substantial improvement. As an example of a substantial improvement, the decree names precisely the addition of automation or artificial intelligence features.

What is **not** covered is custom software development. So if your project is "let's build an AI agent that screens CVs", the agent logic itself is not on the list.

But an agent lives on infrastructure. Cloud virtual machines and databases, storage and backup, encryption, security monitoring: these components can fall into eligible spend, if you buy them from a supplier on the list. That's where the game is played.

## How to structure an AI project around the voucher

The trick isn't to force an ineligible cost. It's to **split the line items** cleanly.

A typical [custom software](/en/software-development) build or [AI agents](/en/ai-agents) sprint has at least three cost blocks:

1. **Infrastructure and security** (EU cloud, databases, backup, encryption, monitoring): eligible for the voucher, if you buy it from a supplier on the list.
2. **Agent / software development** (design, prompts, skills, ATS/ERP integration): outside the voucher, to be covered by other incentives or your own budget.
3. **Adoption and training**: outside the voucher, which explicitly excludes training.

Soraia is not on the list of registered suppliers: you buy block 1 from a supplier that is, and we build the agent on top of that infrastructure.

This structure avoids the most common mistake: a single undifferentiated quote. The application must come with supplier offers carrying the codes of the products and services on the list, and a description of what improves compared with what you use today: a custom development line won't pass there.

## The voucher doesn't justify the project

Here I have to be blunt, because this is where most companies hurt themselves.

A 50% incentive halves the cost of the eligible items, up to a €20,000 contribution. It doesn't create value on its own. If the project has no measurable ROI at full price, it doesn't have one at half price either, you've just wasted half the budget instead of all of it.

Before the voucher you need the same thing we ask for in every sprint: a **measured baseline**. What the process you want to automate costs today, timed on real tasks. Only then do you know whether the agent makes sense.

It's the same principle behind our "first month satisfied or refunded" guarantee: the Sprint Goal is signed at the start of the month and, if the first month doesn't convince you, we refund it or keep working for free until you're satisfied. The voucher reduces the financial risk, the guarantee reduces the operational risk. The two add up, they don't replace each other.

## Window-based means: arrive ready or don't arrive

The MIMIT measure is window-based: applications are reviewed in chronological order and, if the funds run out before 20 January 2027, the window closes early. Each company can submit only one application, and you need an Italian digital identity (SPID, CIE or CNS), a digital signature and certified email (PEC). Whoever reaches 10 November with supplier offers and a completed application has a chance. Whoever "starts thinking about it" afterwards risks finding the till empty.

This applies to many regional and Chamber of Commerce incentives too, which you'll find in our [list of digitalization grants](/bandi) (in Italian), filterable by region. It often pays to map several grants together and figure out which line item goes where.

## When NOT to waste time on the voucher

Straight talk:

- **Spending plan under €4,000**: it isn't eligible. And even just above, weigh the grant against the time it takes to prepare the application.
- **The supplier you want isn't on the list**: the voucher only pays registered suppliers, and registration closed on 27 May 2026.
- **No baseline**: if you don't know what the process costs you today, the voucher is a way to fund a mistake at half price.
- **You need to start now**: only spend after the application counts, and the review normally takes up to 60 days. If you can't wait, start and fund the next project.

Otherwise, [finance](/en/finance) process projects and agent infrastructure are often the most natural candidates, because they have a real, measurable cloud and security component. The eligible part, though, must be bought from a registered supplier, not from us.
