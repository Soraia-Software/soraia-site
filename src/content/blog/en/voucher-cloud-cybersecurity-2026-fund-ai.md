Every year a founder stops me on the same sentence: *"the project makes sense, but there's no budget this year"*.

In 2026 that sentence has a concrete way out. Italy's MIMIT **Cloud and Cybersecurity** voucher covers up to **50% as a non-repayable grant** on eligible spend. And part of your software or AI agent project can fall inside it, if you structure it well.

Careful: it's not a magic shortcut. It's a tool with precise rules. Here's the honest read.

## What is the MIMIT Cloud and Cybersecurity Voucher 2026?

The Cloud and Cybersecurity Voucher 2026 is an incentive from MIMIT (Italy's Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy) aimed at Italian SMEs. It covers up to **50% as a non-repayable grant** on eligible spend for cloud migration, cloud service fees (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) and cybersecurity services and tools, with related consulting and training. The caps and contribution thresholds vary by company size and spending category. The measure is expected to be operational in early 2026, on a first-come (chronological window) basis: funds are assigned in order of arrival until they run out.

**Key takeaways:**
- The Cloud and Cybersecurity voucher 2026 covers up to **50% as a grant** on cloud services, migration and security for SMEs.
- It does not fund "AI agents" as a standalone category: it funds the cloud and cybersecurity components the project is built around.
- It's window-based: whoever arrives with project and quotes ready wins, not whoever starts moving after it opens.
- It's often paired with other incentives (e.g. Piedmont Digitalization Grant 2026, up to 65% grant) to also cover software development.
- An incentive doesn't justify a project without ROI: baseline first, then the application.

## What the voucher actually covers

The MIMIT measure exists to push SMEs toward the cloud and toward a better security posture. In practice it funds:

- Migration of systems and data to cloud infrastructure
- Cloud service fees (eligible IaaS, PaaS, SaaS)
- Cybersecurity services and tools
- Related consulting and training

What is **not** covered directly is pure application software development. So if your project is "let's build an AI agent that screens CVs", the agent logic itself is not a cloud expense.

But an agent lives on infrastructure. EU hosting, data migration, immutable logs, perimeter security: all of these can fall into eligible spend. That's where the game is played.

## How to structure an AI project around the voucher

The trick isn't to force an ineligible cost. It's to **split the line items** cleanly.

A typical [custom software](/en/software-development) build or [AI agents](/en/ai-agents) sprint has at least three cost blocks:

1. **Infrastructure and security** (EU hosting, data migration, encryption, audit logs): eligible for the Cloud and Cybersecurity voucher.
2. **Agent / software development** (design, prompts, skills, ATS/ERP integration): often a better fit for other incentives.
3. **Adoption and training**: sometimes covered, sometimes not, depends on the decree.

This structure avoids the most common mistake: submitting a single undifferentiated quote that the reviewer rejects because "it's not cloud spend".

## The voucher doesn't justify the project

Here I have to be blunt, because this is where most companies hurt themselves.

A 50% incentive halves the cost. It doesn't create value on its own. If the project has no measurable ROI at full price, it doesn't have one at half price either, you've just wasted half the budget instead of all of it.

Before the voucher you need the same thing we ask for in every sprint: a **measured baseline**. What the process you want to automate costs today, timed on real tasks. Only then do you know whether the agent makes sense.

It's the same principle behind our "you pay only if it works" guarantee: the target is written in the contract and measured 30 days after go-live. The voucher reduces the financial risk, the guarantee reduces the operational risk. The two add up, they don't replace each other.

## Window-based means: arrive ready or don't arrive

The MIMIT measure is on a chronological window. Translation: funds run out in order of arrival. Whoever submits with project, quotes and documentation ready on opening day has a chance. Whoever "starts thinking about it" after opening usually finds the till empty.

This applies to other incentives too. If you're a Piedmont SME, for example, the [Piedmont Digitalization Grant 2026](/bando) covers up to 65% as a grant for software, AI agents and automation, also window-based, from 22/10 to 15/12/2026. It often pays to map several grants together and figure out which line item goes where.

## When NOT to waste time on the voucher

Straight talk:

- **Project under €5-10k**: the cost of preparing the application plus the wait often isn't worth the saving.
- **No baseline**: if you don't know what the process costs you today, the voucher is a way to fund a mistake at half price.
- **You need to start now**: if you can't wait for the grant timeline, start and fund the next project.

Otherwise, work on [finance](/en/finance) processes and on agent infrastructure is often the most natural candidate, because it has a real, measurable cloud and security component.
