A manufacturer of awnings and pergolas sells on two fronts. On one side, private buyers, including the leads collected at trade fairs; on the other, dealers, looked after by a network of sales agents who visit them in their territory.

The information sits in different places: trade-fair leads in a spreadsheet, visits in the agents' diaries, quotes in the ERP, dealer terms in a separate file. Whoever runs sales cannot see the whole picture, and that is the first job of a CRM for sales agents and dealers: putting everything on the same data.

**In brief:**
- If you sell to both private buyers and dealers, your data is scattered across trade-fair sheets, agents' diaries and the ERP: one CRM is how you see the whole picture.
- The CRM has to know who brought in each customer and in which territory, because under Article 1748 of the Italian Civil Code an agent's commission can also be due on deals the company closes directly.
- Quotes and commercial terms should be built on the same ERP data, not in a separate file.
- For awnings sold to private buyers in Italy, the ENEA guide updated in September 2026 sets the requirements for the tax deduction and for filing the description of the works within 90 days of their end.
- A CRM like this ships in modules, each tested on its own; the first goes into production in 4 weeks.

## Why a mixed sales network loses information

Private buyers and dealers are two different ways of selling. A private buyer makes one purchase, with a custom quote; a dealer buys all year round, on its own commercial terms. Agents visit the dealers in their territory and collect requests.

When these worlds live in different tools, information gets lost. For example:

- a trade-fair lead reaches nobody, or reaches two people;
- an agent visits a dealer without knowing it has a quote on hold;
- a quote goes out on outdated terms, because the current file was on someone else's computer;
- at year end, nobody can say which fairs brought in sales.

## Territories, customers and commissions: what Italian law says

With a network of agents, a CRM is not just a sales tool: it records facts that money depends on.

[Article 1743 of the Italian Civil Code](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto:1942-03-16;262~art1743) sets out exclusivity: the company cannot use more than one agent at the same time in the same territory and for the same line of business.

[Article 1748](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto:1942-03-16;262~art1748) states when commission is due:

- on deals concluded **as a result of the agent's work**;
- also on deals the company concludes **directly** with customers the agent had previously acquired, or that belong to the **territory or customer group reserved** to the agent, unless agreed otherwise;
- in some cases even on deals concluded **after the contract ends**, if the proposal arrived before or the deal is mainly due to the agent's work.

In practice: if a customer in an agent's reserved territory buys directly from the company, commission may be due to the agent, depending on the contract. Answering that takes data: who brought in the customer, when, in which territory and for which customer group. The CRM has to know from the first contact. ## Private buyers and the awning tax deduction

With private buyers in Italy, the tax deduction comes into play too. The [ENEA guide to solar shading](https://www.efficienzaenergetica.enea.it/images/detrazioni/VADEMECUM_2026/345_SCHERMATURE%20SOLARI.pdf), from Italy's national energy agency and updated on 23 September 2026, sets a few points worth having on the customer record:

- awnings qualify if they face **from east to west through south**: those facing north, north-east and north-west are excluded;
- the shading must have a total solar transmittance **gtot of no more than 0.35**;
- the description of the works must be sent to ENEA **within 90 days** of the end of works;
- for expenses incurred in 2025 and 2026 the deduction is **50% on the main home** and 36% on other homes; for those incurred in 2027 it drops to 36% and 30%.

This data belongs in the CRM, next to the quote: orientation, product, and the end-of-works date the 90 days run from. For pergolas, the requirements have to be checked case by case, because the guide covers solar shading, not pergolas.

## When you don't need a custom CRM

Straight talk, before you spend anything:

- **Small network, few dealers**: an off-the-shelf CRM, well configured, is enough. The comparison is in [custom CRM vs HubSpot or Salesforce](/en/blog/custom-crm-vs-hubspot-salesforce/).
- **Quotes don't touch the ERP**: if terms are simple and the price list rarely changes, the integration costs more than it returns.
- **Agents won't use it**: a CRM nobody updates is worse than a shared spreadsheet. Before building it, find out with your agents what they are willing to log after a visit.

If private buyers and dealers coexist, trade fairs bring leads that get lost and quotes are redone by hand, the numbers add up quickly.
