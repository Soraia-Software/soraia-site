In Italy every company must appoint an RSPP, the head of its prevention and protection service, and many hire an external one. An external safety officer follows many companies, and for each one medical checks, refresher courses, fire extinguisher checks and certifications expire on different dates. One missed deadline is enough to expose the client to a fine, and in the client's eyes the blame falls on whoever was supposed to remember it.

Tracking safety compliance deadlines in a tool built for people who follow several companies solves exactly this: every deadline has its own alert, the safety officer sees every client, and each company sees only its own.

**In brief:**
- For an external safety officer or an HSE consultancy, clients' safety deadlines are the core of the service, and they often live in separate spreadsheets.
- A multi-company deadline tracker gives every medical check, course, extinguisher check and certification its own alert: the safety officer sees all clients, each company only its own.
- The legal references differ by deadline type: periodic medical checks are as a rule yearly (Article 41 of Legislative Decree 81/2008), training follows the State-Regions Agreement of 17 April 2025, and extinguishers need a periodic check at least every six months.
- A tablet inspection app with a checklist and legal references produces the report at the end of the visit, and the same data can feed the risk assessment document.
- The first production release ships in 4 weeks.

## Why do spreadsheets break down with many clients?

While the companies you follow are few, one sheet per client works. Then clients grow, and the problems are always the same:

- deadlines sit in different files, and nobody sees them all together;
- the reminder depends on the memory of whoever opens the file;
- the client can't see their own status and asks for updates by email;
- when a colleague is on holiday, nobody checks their deadlines.

The point is not to digitize the sheet, but to move responsibility for the reminder from the person to the system.

## How a multi-company deadline tracker works

For a safety training and RSPP firm, we are building the platform in which every medical check, course and extinguisher deadline has its own alert. The main features:

- **deadlines for medical checks, courses, extinguishers and certifications**, each with its own alert;
- **three ways of working**: the safety officer who follows several companies, external safety officers, and the in-house safety officer;
- **separate visibility**: the safety officer sees all clients, each company only its own;
- **course sales** in the same platform.

The AI opportunity is specific: reading training certificates and reports to extract dates and deadlines, so they don't have to be entered by hand. It is the next step, not the starting point.

## Which deadlines to track, and under which rules?

| Deadline | Reference | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Periodic medical checks | Article 41 of [Legislative Decree 81/2008](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2008-04-09;81) | as a rule once a year, unless the occupational physician sets a different frequency based on the risk assessment |
| Training and refreshers | [State-Regions Agreement of 17 April 2025](https://lavoro.gov.it/temi-e-priorita/salute-e-sicurezza/focus/pagine/accordo-stato-regioni-del-17042025-materia-di-formazione) | in force since 24 May 2025, it sets durations and refreshers for each role |
| Fire extinguishers | [Ministerial Decree of 1 September 2021](https://www.vigilfuoco.it/servizi-le-aziende-e-i-professionisti/formazione-abilitazione-e-aggiornamento-addetti-antincendio-e-docentiformatori/dm-settembre-2021-e-decreto-controlli) and the UNI 9994-1 standard | periodic check at least every six months |
| Certifications and licences | depending on the equipment or activity | each with its own expiry and renewal |

A useful tracker doesn't stop at the date: for every deadline it says who owns it, which company it concerns and what it takes to close it.

## From site inspection to report, without retyping

Deadlines are half the job. The other half is the site inspection, which many firms still do with a paper checklist and a report rewritten at the office, sometimes days later.

For a safety and environmental consultancy, we designed a tablet app that changes this step:

- a **complete checklist**, with the legal references and the recommended action for each item;
- **photos taken on site**;
- **separate roles** for the technician and the firm's owner;
- a **report ready at the end of the inspection**, generated from the data collected.

The next step is the risk assessment document required by Articles 28 and 29 of Legislative Decree 81/2008, generated from the same data with the client's template. The firm's stated goal is simple: assess more companies a month with the same team.

## Where AI helps, and where it isn't needed

In both projects the main value is not AI: it is having the right data in the right place, entered once. Reports are generated from the data collected, with no language model required. AI becomes useful when documents arrive from outside, such as certificates and reports; in the inspection project it is an optional module that the firm has chosen not to switch on for now.

## When you don't need custom software

- **You follow few clients** and a shared calendar is enough to miss nothing.
- **A vertical safety software already covers your workflows**: use it. We say so in our comparison of [vertical SaaS and custom builds](/en/confronto/vertical-saas-vs-custom-ai-agent/).

Custom makes sense when your value lies in how you work, in your own report and risk assessment templates: then the software adapts to your method, not the other way round.
