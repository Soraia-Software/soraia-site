CV screening is the silent bottleneck of almost every SMB that hires at volume. Applications arrive by the hundreds, the inbox fills up, good candidates wait and leave. The question for a Head of HR isn't "is AI the future", but: what does an AI agent actually change in my recruitment process, and how do I verify it?

Honest answer from someone who builds these agents: it changes the sorting hours, not the hiring decisions. And it's only worth it where there's volume.

**Key takeaways:**
- An AI agent in recruitment attacks the high-volume work: CV screening, candidate contact at scale, job description parsing, interview scheduling.
- It doesn't replace the recruiter: it takes the sorting off their plate and leaves them the decisions.
- Real numbers from our cases: 100k+ candidates handled (about 4 extra recruiters), 20k+ candidates with under 1 minute response time.
- It pays off where volume is high and repetitive; on rare, highly varied hiring it doesn't justify a custom build.
- Without a timed baseline and a single primary metric, you can't say whether it worked.

## Where time is lost in SMB recruitment

Before talking about agents, look at where your HR team's time actually goes. In almost every SMB that hires at volume, time concentrates in three places:

1. **Incoming CV screening.** Hundreds of applications per role, most off-target, read one by one.
2. **Candidate contact and follow-up.** Replying, rescheduling, chasing. Repetitive and high-volume.
3. **Interview coordination.** Fitting calendars between candidate and hiring manager.

This is exactly the work an AI agent can absorb. Not the final evaluation, not the negotiation: the sorting.

## What an AI agent does (and doesn't do) in recruitment

An AI agent on an HR process does the repetitive work inside your systems: it reads and classifies CVs, parses job descriptions, contacts candidates at scale, manages the interview calendar, does BD on companies in a hiring spike. These are the use cases we cover in the [Recruitment & HR](/en/recruitment) cluster.

What it does NOT do: it doesn't decide who to hire for you. The agent brings the recruiter a qualified shortlist and context; the decision stays human. If someone sells you an agent that "hires on its own", you're buying a risk, not a result.

## Real numbers from our cases (not projections)

This is what a Head of HR needs: what actually happened, in published cases.

- **[APraise](/en/case-studies/apraise)**, executive search boutique: the AI agent handled **100k+ candidates**, equivalent to about **4 extra recruiters**, without growing headcount.
- **[Stars Be Original](/en/case-studies/stars-be-original)**, hospitality recruitment: **20k+ candidates** handled by the agent, with **under 1 minute** response time.
- **[Talent Match](/en/case-studies/talent-match)**: the "Braint" recruitment platform, with a custom ATS and CRM plus AI candidate matching.

These are published results. Your process will produce different numbers, which is why the baseline isn't optional.

## How to verify it worked

Without a baseline, every savings claim is a meeting-room opinion. The bare minimum, for recruitment:

- **Timed baseline:** real time on 10-20 applications or coordinated interviews, not "the team spends a morning on it".
- **One primary metric:** hours per person per week on CV sorting, or time-to-first-contact on the candidate. Just one.
- **A clear window:** you measure at 30 days post go-live, not before. Earlier you're only measuring adoption noise.

It's precisely because we build this measurement that we can hold the **you only pay if it works** guarantee: the metric target is written into the contract. If we miss it, we work for free until we hit it, or we refund.

## When it is NOT worth it

Straight to your face, because on HR it's easy to spend badly:
- **Rare, highly varied hiring:** few hires a year with always-different profiles don't justify a custom agent. Off-the-shelf tools plus some process discipline are better.
- **A process that changes every quarter:** if the selection flow is unstable, you'll be rebuilding the agent constantly.
- **No appetite to measure:** without a baseline no one can guarantee you anything, and you should distrust anyone who does.

The agent pays off where volume is high and repetitive: hundreds of applications, recurring screening, seasonal hiring spikes.
