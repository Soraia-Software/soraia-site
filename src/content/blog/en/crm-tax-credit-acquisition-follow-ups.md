In Italy's tax credit market, negotiations drag on. Whoever is selling the credit compares offers, waits for the bank's answer, puts things off. Whoever calls back at the right moment, with the right offer, closes; whoever calls at random annoys people, or arrives too late.

A CRM for buying tax credits is mostly about one thing: knowing every morning who to call, why now and what to say. Pipeline, history and follow-ups all serve that list.

**In brief:**
- When buying tax credits, negotiations are long and close by calling back at the right moment: the agenda matters as much as the price.
- The pipeline should be organised by credit status, because the rules depend on the credit's history: under Italian law, after the first transfer a credit can only move on in limited cases, through banks, financial intermediaries and insurers (Article 121 of Decree-Law 34/2020).
- An annual instalment of a credit not used within the year is lost and cannot be refunded: in the CRM, the date matters as much as the amount.
- AI prepares the call agenda with a reason, an opening line and questions, and writes the follow-ups and the draft contract; decisions stay with a person.
- On WhatsApp you may only message people who gave you their number and agreed to receive messages: WhatsApp's Business Policy requires it.

## Why calling back wins when buying credits

A negotiation to buy a credit does not close in one phone call. The seller, a company or a private individual, compares several offers, has to gather documents and sometimes waits to see whether the bank will buy on better terms.

The salesperson's job turns into remembering: who asked to talk again after the bank's answer, who was offered what, who promised a document. If that memory lives in an Excel sheet and phone chats, someone slips through, and it may be exactly the person who was about to accept.

## What we designed

For a company that buys tax credits from businesses and private individuals, we designed a CRM built around the call agenda:

1. **A pipeline by credit status**, not by generic sales stage.
2. **History of conversations and offers** for each contact, to reread before every call.
3. **Daily agenda**: every morning AI prepares who to call back, the reason to do it now, an opening line and the questions to ask.
4. **Automatic follow-ups**, first by email and then on WhatsApp, written by AI from that contact's context.
5. **Draft contract and document list** when the negotiation reaches the end.

Follow-ups go out on their own, but within precise rules: when to send and on which channel.

## Credit status: what Italian law says

A credit buyer is not buying a contact: they are buying a credit with a history. Three rules explain why the pipeline should be built on credit status.

**No new options, except for exemptions.** Since 17 February 2023, [Decree-Law 11/2023](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legge:2023-02-16;11~art2) no longer allows new invoice-discount or credit-transfer options for building bonuses, except for the exemptions it lists. So the market works on credits that already exist, each with its own history.

**Limited, traceable transfers.** [Article 121 of Decree-Law 34/2020](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legge:2020-05-19;34~art121) allows the credit to be transferred to other parties, including non-financial ones; after the first transfer, though, it can only move on in limited cases, through banks, registered financial intermediaries, banking group companies and insurers. For notices sent from 1 May 2022, each credit also has a unique identification code and, after the first notice, cannot be transferred in parts.

**This year's instalment expires with the year.** The credit is used to offset taxes through the F24 payment form, in the same annual instalments as the original deduction. The share not used within the year cannot be used in later years or refunded, says paragraph 3 of the same article.

In practice, each credit's record in the CRM should show who holds it, whether it has already been transferred, its identification code, the remaining instalments and the year of each. That data is what gives the reason for the call.

## Where AI helps, and where it does not

AI works where time is lost today: rereading the history before each call, deciding the order of calls, writing follow-ups that don't all sound the same, preparing the draft contract and the document list.

Decisions stay with a person: whether to buy, at what price, with which guarantees. A system that suggests the order of calls can get it wrong without much harm; one that sets prices cannot. Who is liable when an agent gets it wrong is covered in [who pays when an AI agent fails](/en/blog/who-pays-when-ai-agent-fails-liability-smb/).

## WhatsApp: consent first

A WhatsApp follow-up is the most direct, and that is exactly why it has strict rules. [WhatsApp's Business Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) lets you contact someone only if they gave you their number and confirmed they want to receive your messages. It requires you to respect every request to stop, and leaves it to you to collect consent in line with applicable law.

In the CRM, consent is data like any other: present or absent, with its date. Without consent, the follow-up stays on email. If you are weighing Meta's bot against a custom agent, the comparison is in [Meta's WhatsApp Business Agent](/en/blog/whatsapp-business-agent-meta-italy-smb/).

## When you don't need a custom CRM

Straight talk, before you spend anything:

- **Few contacts and short negotiations**: an off-the-shelf CRM with reminders does the job. The comparison is in [custom CRM vs HubSpot or Salesforce](/en/blog/custom-crm-vs-hubspot-salesforce/).
- **You don't track credit status**: without that data the agenda has no reasons to suggest. Start there.
- **You buy few credits, always from the same sellers**: the relationship matters more than the system.

If you have many open negotiations that drag on and get lost for want of a call back, the numbers add up quickly.
